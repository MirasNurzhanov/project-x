This describes what Approx collects, why, and what control you have. It is a companion to the Terms of Use and the Community Guidelines.

What Approx collects

  • Account — username, email address, date of birth, and anything you choose to put on your profile.
  • Location — latitude and longitude reported by your device, only while you have chosen to share it.
  • Content — Swifts, stories, comments, messages, and the images you attach to them.
  • Social graph — your friends, connections, and your block list.
  • Safety — reports you file, reports filed about you, blocks, and moderation decisions.
  • Technical — device type, app version, server logs and IP address.

Location, in detail

This is the part worth reading twice.

  • Every account starts Paused. While Paused, your position is used only to answer your own searches — it is never stored on our servers and never shown to anybody.
  • You appear on other people's maps only after you choose Live or Approximate yourself, on a screen explaining what that means. Granting the operating system's location permission does not do this.
  • Approximate rounds your position to a grid roughly 500m across before it is stored, so a precise position never reaches our database at all.
  • Sharing ends when you choose Paused or close the app. It does not persist across launches: you choose again each time.
  • If you allow background location, Approx keeps your position current while the app is closed only while you are sharing. Choosing Paused stops it immediately, and this permission is optional — everything works while the app is open without it.
  • People you have blocked never see you, in any mode.

Who receives your data

Other Approx users see what you chose to show them, at the audience you chose. Beyond that: our hosting and storage providers, and law enforcement where we are legally required or where there is a credible threat to somebody's safety. We do not sell your data and we do not read your private messages except when responding to a specific abuse report or a valid legal obligation.

Retention and deletion

Deleting your account from Settings removes your profile, content and stored position. Where an account has been reported, blocked or removed for abuse we may keep the safety record — the report, the content it concerned, and the decision — because deleting it would erase the evidence that a decision was correct.

Your controls

  • Location sharing: Live, Approximate or Paused, changeable at any moment.
  • Per-field visibility for interests, stories, stats, connections, links and presence.
  • A private account, so only approved people see anything beyond your name and picture.
  • Discoverability switches for search by username and by interest.
  • Block any account. Blocking is immediate and mutual.
  • Report any content or account. Reports are reviewed by a person.
  • Delete your account outright.

Children

Approx is not for anybody under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child is using Approx, write to {{ contact_email }} with the username; we will verify, delete the account and erase its data.

Staying safe

Before you meet anyone

  • Meet in a public, populated place the first time — somewhere with staff and other people around, in daylight.
  • Tell a friend where you are going, who you are meeting, and when you expect to be back.
  • Arrange your own transport there and back, so leaving is always entirely your decision.
  • Chat inside the app first. There is no rush to move to another platform or to hand over your phone number, address or workplace.
  • Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, leave. You owe a stranger no explanation.

Protecting yourself in the app

  • Keep identifying details out of your bio — your home street, your school or workplace, your daily routine, or a photo that shows your front door.
  • Use Paused when you are somewhere you would rather not be locatable, such as at home or at work.
  • Remember that anything you send in a chat can be screenshotted by the person receiving it.
  • Never send money, gift cards or account credentials to someone you met on Approx. Requests for those are the clearest sign of a scam.

If someone makes you uncomfortable

  • Block them. It takes effect immediately and stops them seeing you or contacting you anywhere in Approx.
  • Report them from their profile or from the content itself. Reports are reviewed by a person.
  • Preserve the evidence before deleting a conversation — deleting it removes your copy too.
  • Contact your local emergency services if you are in immediate danger. Do that first; reporting to us can wait.

Changes

Approx is actively developed, so this will change as the app does. When the substance changes we publish a new version and ask you to agree to it in the app.
