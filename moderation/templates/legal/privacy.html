This describes what Approx collects, why, and what control you have. It is a companion to the Terms of Use and the Community Guidelines.

What Approx collects

  • Account — username, email address, date of birth, and anything you choose to put on your profile.
  • Location — the precise latitude and longitude your device reports, stored while you are not in Ghost Mode, plus how accurate that fix was. What other people are shown is decided per viewer; see below.
  • Content — Swifts, stories, comments, messages, and the images you attach to them.
  • Social graph — your friends, connections, and your block list.
  • Safety — reports you file, reports filed about you, blocks, and moderation decisions.
  • Technical — device type, app version, server logs and IP address.

Location, in detail

This is the part worth reading twice, and it changed in September 2026. If you read an earlier version of this policy, read this section again — what your device sends us is not what it used to be.

  • There is one control, Ghost Mode, and it is off by default: an ordinary account is visible on the map. Turning Ghost Mode on takes you off it. There is no longer a Live / Approximate / Paused choice.
  • Sharing persists between sessions. It does not reset when you close Approx. It stays as you left it until you change it.
  • Nothing is transmitted until you have been shown a screen explaining what being on the map means, once per installation. Until you have seen it your device sends no position at all, and an account with no position is on nobody's map. Granting the operating system's location permission does not by itself put you on anybody's map.
  • Your device sends your precise position, and we store it. This is a deliberate change and it is a real widening of what leaves your phone. Approx used to round your coordinates on the device before sending them, so a precise position never reached us. It does now, because your friends are entitled to see where you actually are and your phone cannot know who will ask.
  • Precision is decided per viewer, by us, when they look — not by a setting you pick:
    • You, and people you are friends with, see your precise position.
    • Everybody else sees it snapped to a grid cell roughly 500 metres across.
  • That snapping is deterministic and the grid is offset differently for every account. Watching a stranger repeatedly does not narrow their position down: every reading inside a cell returns the same coordinate, and there is no shared grid whose cell centres are public knowledge. The distance shown to someone who is not your friend is measured to that same cell, not to where you really are.
  • A position goes stale after about fifteen minutes. Once it does you are off the map until your device reports a newer one — including if Approx was force-closed or lost connectivity.
  • Ghost Mode is mutual. While it is on you are on nobody's map and nobody is on yours. It does not delete the position we hold, which still answers features that are only about you, such as which clubs and region you belong to — but no other person receives it.
  • People you have blocked never see you, and you never see them. That applies in both directions and regardless of any other setting.
  • Background location is optional and separate. If you allow it, Approx keeps your position current while the app is closed, and only while you are sharing. Turning on Ghost Mode stops it. Everything else works without it.
  • Two things ride alongside a position. Speed is sorted into a coarse class — roughly, whether you are still, walking or moving quickly — and the number your phone reported is discarded; no numeric speed is ever stored or shown to anyone. Accuracy (how well your phone knows where it is) is stored, because a fix with a two-kilometre margin cannot answer which district you are standing in; it says nothing about where you are.

Who receives your data

Other Approx users see what you chose to show them, at the audience you chose. Beyond that: our hosting and storage providers, and law enforcement where we are legally required or where there is a credible threat to somebody's safety. We do not sell your data and we do not read your private messages except when responding to a specific abuse report or a valid legal obligation.

Retention and deletion

A stored position is replaced by each newer one and is not kept as a history of where you have been; once it is about fifteen minutes old it stops being published to anyone. Ghost Mode hides you but does not delete it. Deleting your account from Settings removes your profile, content and stored position. Where an account has been reported, blocked or removed for abuse we may keep the safety record — the report, the content it concerned, and the decision — because deleting it would erase the evidence that a decision was correct.

Your controls

  • Ghost Mode, which takes you off the map entirely and is mutual. Changeable at any moment.
  • Per-field visibility for interests, stories, stats, connections, links and presence.
  • A private account, so only approved people see anything beyond your name and picture.
  • Discoverability switches for search by username and by interest.
  • Block any account. Blocking is immediate and mutual.
  • Report any content or account. Reports are reviewed by a person.
  • Delete your account outright.

Children

Approx is not for anybody under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child is using Approx, write to {{ contact_email }} with the username; we will verify, delete the account and erase its data.

Staying safe

Before you meet anyone

  • Meet in a public, populated place the first time — somewhere with staff and other people around, in daylight.
  • Tell a friend where you are going, who you are meeting, and when you expect to be back.
  • Arrange your own transport there and back, so leaving is always entirely your decision.
  • Chat inside the app first. There is no rush to move to another platform or to hand over your phone number, address or workplace.
  • Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, leave. You owe a stranger no explanation.

Protecting yourself in the app

  • Keep identifying details out of your bio — your home street, your school or workplace, your daily routine, or a photo that shows your front door.
  • Use Paused when you are somewhere you would rather not be locatable, such as at home or at work.
  • Remember that anything you send in a chat can be screenshotted by the person receiving it.
  • Never send money, gift cards or account credentials to someone you met on Approx. Requests for those are the clearest sign of a scam.

If someone makes you uncomfortable

  • Block them. It takes effect immediately and stops them seeing you or contacting you anywhere in Approx.
  • Report them from their profile or from the content itself. Reports are reviewed by a person.
  • Preserve the evidence before deleting a conversation — deleting it removes your copy too.
  • Contact your local emergency services if you are in immediate danger. Do that first; reporting to us can wait.

Changes

Approx is actively developed, so this will change as the app does. When the substance changes we publish a new version and ask you to agree to it in the app.
